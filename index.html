Research direction

Machine learning can predict a material's properties in milliseconds instead of hours of computation. My work asks when those predictions can be trusted, and builds the checks that tell.

  1. 1

    Generalization

    Does a model hold up on chemistry it has never seen? Out-of-distribution testing of graph neural networks, and transfer of pretrained interatomic potentials to new properties.

  2. 2

    Screening

    Can a cheap ML signal decide which materials deserve an expensive calculation? Judged against thousands of first-principles transport calculations.

  3. 3

    Experiment

    Do calculations agree with measurement? Finding and correcting the bias that ML surrogates inherit from the calculations they are trained on.

Research

CurrentNIMS, Tsukuba · Spin Theory Group of Prof. Terumasa Tadano · Sep – Dec 2026

Discovering new alkali- and alkaline-earth Heusler thermoelectrics with pretrained AI models.

New Heusler compositions built combinatorially and ranked by their predicted Seebeck coefficient and lattice thermal conductivity, using transfer learning from pretrained machine-learned interatomic potentials.

The idea
  • Universal interatomic potentials are pretrained on over a hundred million DFT calculations to predict energies and forces; to do that, their inner layers must already encode each atom's chemical environment.
  • The direction: reuse those learned representations for transport properties, following the frozen-transfer-learning approach of Xiao and Tadano (npj Comput. Mater. 2026).
  • Two architectures: eSEN (equivariant convolution) and EquiformerV3 (equivariant attention).

Work in progress.

PreprintSVNIT Surat · 2026 · arXiv:2610.18179 · code

The density of states can choose what to calculate; only a band structure shows whether a gap exists.

ML-predicted densities of states for 106,235 Heusler structures, used as a thermoelectric screen and judged against 3,625 first-principles transport calculations.

≈4×fewer DFT runs per confirmed candidate
80candidates found, 61 never measured
10 / 11known half-Heusler thermoelectrics pass the same checks
0.15gap recall of ML spectra, hidden by high correlation
How it works
  • Trained 30 ALIGNN graph neural networks on 51,257 DFT d-orbital spectra (H100 GPUs); held-out Pearson r = 0.90 against 0.49–0.61 for baselines.
  • A Seebeck indicator read from the spectrum ranks the compounds; Quantum ESPRESSO + BoltzTraP2 calculations, including 607 spin–orbit reruns, decide which pass.
  • Calculating only the top fifth by the indicator cuts DFT runs per confirmed candidate from about 21 to 5.5, while keeping three quarters of them.
  • Wrote the whole first-principles workflow, including a BoltzTraP2 patch for spin–orbit runs.

ManuscriptSVNIT Surat · 2026 · data & code

Published calculations overestimate measured thermal conductivity by a median 1.69×. Correcting that lets ML predict experiment.

A provenance-tracked corpus of lattice thermal conductivity for half-Heuslers, a calculation-to-experiment correction, and an ML model validated against measurement.

33%median error vs. 46–47% for baselines
9,495κL values for 960 compounds
101 → 37%error of an independent ML force field, corrected
10testable predictions with 90% intervals
How it works
  • Built the corpus from five databases and the literature, including 1,178 multimodal-LLM (Gemini 2.5) extractions, each gated by physical checks.
  • Showed that published calculations exceed measurement in 29 of 34 half-Heuslers; a two-parameter transfer function corrects it.
  • CatBoost surrogate plus correction, validated with each chemistry withheld from training, for cubic 18-electron compounds; the typical error matches the spread between two laboratories.

ReportS. N. Bose Centre, Kolkata · SRFP 2025 · report

High accuracy on familiar data is not discovery.

Specialist graph neural networks fell from over 92% to about 48% accuracy on unseen double perovskites; a broadly pretrained model held about 80%.

How it works
  • Built an end-to-end ALIGNN pipeline, from CIF files to atom and line graphs to training and inference.
  • Compared a pretrained general model with two specialists on metallicity classification of an out-of-distribution set; traced the gap to the breadth of training data.
  • Supervised by Prof. Tanusri Saha-Dasgupta.
